My Views on Generative AI

Don't use AI to write code before you know how to write it yourself!

I believe generative AI can have its uses, but it should be much more limited than the current scope of things we are using it for.

As a student, I understand the temptation of reaching for the quicker option to finish assignments and be able to do literally anything else besides homework, but the reason I became a computer science major in the first place was because I like to code. I like to learn new things, try them out, struggle to make it work, then finally get the payoff of seeing the results. I don't want to be just another developer, I want to be a good one; and to make that happen I need to learn things the hard way without AI.

There's no way to check the quality of the code AI outputs if you haven't first learned it yourself. This is why I have decided to never use AI to write my code for me to complete assignments. All the work you see on this website is entirely my own.

Now, as I said, there are some uses for AI that I do think it's worth using. Mainly, I use it for debugging. Show it the code, tell me what might have gone wrong, then research how to fix it myself and test again.


My Story

I've lived in SoCal my whole life and somehow go to the beach very rarely. I joined my high school FRC robotics team in my freshman year, and it was there that I first learned to code in Java. My friend and I sat in the corner of the engineering classroom most of that year struggling together through the AP Java course we were given. Despite the struggle, it was the first time I realized I enjoyed coding.

After graduating high school, I went to my local community college and finished all my general education and lower division computer science classes. After 2 years there, I transferred to UCSD, where I was unpleasantly suprised by the monster known as the quarter system. Now in my second year at UCSD, I can confidently say that it has been the most work I've done in my life, but also the most rewarding. I have learned incomparable amounts of knowledge here with hands-on work, long nights studying, and struggling through with friends.

All the experience I have gathered has led me to become IEEE's webmaster. From the web dev experience I got last quarter from CSE 110 and the WatchTower project, to immediately applying that knowledge to my team's IEEE spring quarterly project, it has expanded my knowledge enough to give me the confidence that I can handle managing UCSD IEEE's website.

Now I am diving deeper into web development this quarter with my classes CSE134b, Intro to Web Development, and CSE190, How the Web Works.
